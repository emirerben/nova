# Kria social kit

Outro, watermark and reusable clip templates for the TikTok / Reels / Shorts
accounts (`@usekria`, `@kria.football`, `@kria.travel`, `@kria.vlog`).

Everything in `dist/` is generated by `build.py` from two in-repo sources of
truth — the wordmark outlines in `src/apps/web/public/favicon.svg` and the
brand fonts in `src/apps/api/assets/fonts/`. Nothing is hand-drawn, so the kit
cannot drift away from the product. **Do not hand-edit anything in `dist/`;
change `build.py` and rebuild.**

```bash
# from the repo root, using the API virtualenv (it has skia-python + numpy)
src/apps/api/.venv/bin/python brand/social/build.py
```

The build exits non-zero if any shipped placement collides with app UI or any
signed-off variant drops below the contrast floor. That exit code is the gate —
treat a red build the same way you would a failing test.

## 1. Safe placement

All coordinates are for a 1080×1920 export.

TikTok, Reels and Shorts each cover part of the frame with their own UI. The
map lives in `kria_brand.OCCLUSION` and is drawn in
[`dist/proofs/safezone-map.png`](dist/proofs/safezone-map.png).

> ⚠️ **Open item — the map is not yet device-verified.** The rectangles come
> from the platforms' published safe-area guidance, not from screenshots on our
> own phones. They are deliberately conservative, but chrome shifts with device
> and app version. Before a launch push, capture a full-screen frame in each app
> on the team's handsets, compare against the map, and correct `OCCLUSION`.
> Everything else recomputes from it.

**The safe rectangle is `x 60 → 890`, `y 200 → 1520`.** It is clear on all three
platforms at once. Anything you need a viewer to read goes inside it.

Two useful exceptions:

- Above `y = 980` no platform has a right action rail, so the top band is wider:
  `x 60 → 1020`, `y 200 → 980`.
- Below `y = 1530` you are in the caption / username / audio block on every
  platform. Never put text there, and never assume your own caption burn-in
  will survive it. **1530 is the floor, and the watermark sits right on it** —
  the mark is anchored by its bottom edge, so all three sizes end at 1530 and
  none of them creeps under the username. `build.py place <video>` draws the
  chrome over a real frame if you want to see it.

| | x | y |
| --- | --- | --- |
| watermark, primary (bottom-left) | 60 | bottom edge pinned to **1530** |
| watermark, alternate (bottom-left is busy) | 60 | 210 |
| hook title block | 60 | 255 → 700 (kicker + 3 lines) |
| step chip | 60 | 1294 → 1400 |

These are not aspirations: the build measures the ink actually drawn in each
template, checks that rectangle against the chrome map, and records both in
`placements` in [`dist/proofs/report.json`](dist/proofs/report.json). The
measured rectangles are shadow-inclusive, so they feather a few pixels past the
safe rectangle on the widest lines while the glyphs themselves stay inside.

> **Re-measure when an app reflows its UI.** If TikTok moves the rail, update
> `OCCLUSION` and rebuild; everything downstream re-checks itself.

The legibility numbers in §6 are a different matter — those are measured
directly, by compositing the real assets and reading the pixels.

## 2. Watermark

`dist/watermark/kria-watermark-<variant>-<size>.png`

Three sizes — `compact` (140px mark), `standard` (168px), `demo` (210px).
Use `standard` unless you have a reason. `demo` is for product walkthroughs
where the watermark doubles as the title; `compact` is for busy travel edits.

Opacity is already baked into every file. Do not add your own on top.

### Which variant

The mark is a quiet grey with a diffuse shadow and nothing else — no chip, no
halo, no box. Two tones cover the range:

| variant | use on | worst measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `mist` `#CAD2DB` @85% | **default** — dark, mid-tone, warm or busy footage | 2.2:1 |
| `graphite` `#526071` @92% | bright, pale or overexposed footage | 4.5:1 |
| `sky` `#9BCAFF` | white product screens and UI demos only — the brand logotype, never over footage | n/a |

`mist` is the look; `graphite` is the same treatment re-toned so the mark does
not vanish on a bright shot. Switching between them is the only decision an
editor has to make, and `build.py pick` makes it for you.

If you are unsure, don't guess — measure the actual shot:

```bash
src/apps/api/.venv/bin/python brand/social/build.py pick myclip.mp4 --at 2.0
```

It composites every variant onto that frame, prints the contrast each one
achieves, and names the file to use.

### Dropping it on

The PNGs carry a transparent margin for the shadow, so the overlay origin is
**not** the placement origin. Every file's exact `overlay_xy` is in
[`dist/manifest.json`](dist/manifest.json). For `mist`/`standard`/bottom-left:

```bash
ffmpeg -i clip.mp4 -i brand/social/dist/watermark/kria-watermark-mist-standard.png \
  -filter_complex "[0:v][1:v]overlay=30:1425" \
  -c:v libx264 -preset slow -crf 18 -pix_fmt yuv420p -c:a copy out.mp4
```

In CapCut, import the PNG as an overlay, set scale to 100% and pin it to the
bottom-left with the frame's own margins — do not eyeball a different size.

## 3. Outro

1.6 s, 1080×1920, 30 fps. The letters land in the approved `k · r · i · a`
rhythm and hold. The mark carries the card on its own — no URL, no tagline.

| file | use |
| --- | --- |
| `dist/outro/kria-outro-paper.mp4` | **append** to the end of a clip. White canvas, Sky wordmark. Carries a silent stereo AAC track so concatenating onto a clip that has audio does not drop the audio stream. |
| `dist/outro/kria-outro-overlay.webm` | **overlay** on your last shot (VP9 + alpha). Footage stays visible under a wash that fades in, white wordmark on top. Use this on footage-led viral edits where a hard cut to white would kill the watch-through. |
| `dist/outro/kria-endcard-*.png` | the final frame as a still, for thumbnails or for editors who prefer a static card |

```bash
# append the paper outro
ffmpeg -i clip.mp4 -i brand/social/dist/outro/kria-outro-paper.mp4 \
  -filter_complex "[0:v]scale=1080:1920,setsar=1,fps=30[v0];\
[1:v]scale=1080:1920,setsar=1,fps=30[v1];[v0][v1]concat=n=2:v=1[v];\
[0:a][1:a]concat=n=2:v=0:a=1[a]" -map "[v]" -map "[a]" \
  -c:v libx264 -preset slow -crf 18 -pix_fmt yuv420p -c:a aac out.mp4
```

Need ProRes 4444 for Premiere or Resolve? `build.py --prores` writes
`kria-outro-overlay.mov` locally. It is gitignored on purpose — it is ~50× the
size of the WebM and regenerates in seconds.

## 4. Clip templates

`dist/templates/` holds rendered examples. Render your own with the same
geometry and type:

```bash
src/apps/api/.venv/bin/python brand/social/build.py card \
  --kind hook --text "This took 40 seconds" --kicker "day 14" --out hook.png

src/apps/api/.venv/bin/python brand/social/build.py card \
  --kind step --number 2 --text "Kria cuts it to the beat" --out step.png
```

- **hook** — Fraunces Bold, white with a diffuse shadow, top of the safe
  rectangle, wraps to a maximum of three lines. The optional kicker is Butter.
  This is the first 2–3 seconds of a viral clip.
- **step** — Sky numbered chip on a near-white pill, warm ink label, sitting
  just above the caption block. This is the demo/product walkthrough beat.

Burned-in captions are not part of this kit — they come out of the render
pipeline. Keep them inside the same safe rectangle and leave the top 290px for
the hook so the two never fight.

## 5. Rules that keep every account looking like Kria

1. **One wordmark.** It comes from `favicon.svg`. Never retype `kria` in another
   typeface, never re-space the letters, never rotate them yourself. The letter
   rhythm *is* the mark.
2. **One watermark position per account, held for the whole series.**
   Bottom-left, bottom edge on 1530. Moving it clip to clip reads as sloppy,
   not dynamic.
3. **Sky is for brand and selection. Butter is for the one action.** No other
   accent — no gradients, no second colour per video. (`DESIGN.md` §9.)
4. **Fraunces for display, Inter for everything else.** DynaPuff is the wordmark
   only; it never sets a sentence.
5. **Every clip ends on the outro.** Paper for product and demo clips, overlay
   for footage-led ones.
6. **Shadows, never outlines.** Legibility comes from a diffuse shadow or the
   plate scrim, matching how the render pipeline draws text.
7. **Stay in the safe rectangle**, and check a real frame with `build.py pick`
   rather than trusting a corner to stay dark for the whole shot.

## 6. Legibility evidence

Every variant, measured against five footage classes — bright, dark, mid-grey,
warm and high-frequency busy. Contrast is WCAG 2.2, measured between the glyph
and the ring of pixels immediately around it, then split into a 8×3 tile grid so
a mark that reads on average but vanishes over one bright patch still fails.

**The watermark is gated at 2.0:1, not the 3.0:1 used for text.** This is a
deliberate, narrow exception. An unaided grey mark cannot hold 3:1 over busy
mid-tone footage without a chip or a halo behind it — that was measured across
three rounds of candidates — and putting a box behind the wordmark was rejected
as too heavy. A watermark is an attribution mark rather than content: it has to
be *noticeable*, not readable at a glance, and being quiet is the brief. Hook
titles, step labels and captions are still gated at 3.0:1.

Sheets: `dist/proofs/legibility-<variant>-<footage>.jpg` (half-resolution;
they are evidence, not deliverables).

| variant | footage | worst tile | signed off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `mist` | bright | 1.1:1 | no — out of policy |
| `mist` | busy | 2.5:1 | **yes** — pass |
| `mist` | dark | 8.0:1 | **yes** — pass |
| `mist` | mid | 2.6:1 | **yes** — pass |
| `mist` | warm | 2.2:1 | **yes** — pass |
| `graphite` | bright | 4.5:1 | **yes** — pass |
| `graphite` | busy | 1.5:1 | no — out of policy |
| `graphite` | dark | 2.3:1 | no — out of policy |
| `graphite` | mid | 1.4:1 | no — out of policy |
| `graphite` | warm | 1.7:1 | no — out of policy |
| `sky` | bright | 1.5:1 | n/a — logotype |
| `sky` | busy | 2.1:1 | n/a — logotype |
| `sky` | dark | 9.1:1 | n/a — logotype |
| `sky` | mid | 2.3:1 | n/a — logotype |
| `sky` | warm | 1.9:1 | n/a — logotype |

The rows marked *out of policy* are why the two-tone rule exists: `mist` really
does collapse to 1.1:1 on a blown-out sky, and `graphite` to 1.5:1 on a busy
mid-tone crowd. They are measured and kept visible rather than left as folklore.

`sky` is the brand logotype on white product surfaces. WCAG 1.4.11 exempts
logotypes from the contrast minimum, so it is reported but never gated — do not
use it as a watermark over footage.

### Exploring alternatives

`build.py compare <video>` composites a ladder of candidate treatments onto real
frames and measures each one; `build.py place <video>` shows one treatment at
several heights with the chrome drawn over it. Both were used to arrive at the
current mark, and both are the right tool if the treatment is revisited.
