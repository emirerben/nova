# Talk-to-camera green screen (speaker background) — failure modes (written before the code)

Linear: project `Talk-to-camera green-screen backgrounds` (P-KRI-21), phases KRI-1114 to KRI-1120.

Scope of the first cut: one background (image or video, muted, looping) behind the
speaker for the whole edit, on device-rendered Talk-to-camera (`subtitled`) edits.
Entry points: live camera preview, editor Background tool, chat (strategy + ops).
Cloud talking_head variants, the web editor, windowed or per-clip backgrounds and
blurred backgrounds are out of scope.

The pinned test names below are the planned names. A row is not done until its
test exists and is green, or it is marked `device (owed)`.

## Decisions that remove whole classes of bugs

- **Segment the speaker layer, not the composited scene.** The text-behind-subject
  matte segments the composited canvas, which is right for hiding text. For a
  background the mask must come from the speaker clip only, after its transform and
  before cutaways, fullscreen cards and overlays, otherwise a cutaway or card would
  be cut out of the picture. The mask still lives in canvas space by construction.
- **Soft alpha, not a hard cut.** The text matte hard-cuts at 0.40. A full-frame
  matte with a hard cut gives blocky, haloed hair. The speaker matte keeps the soft
  confidence and smooths it over time.
- **Failure holds, it never flashes.** The text matte answers "no mask" on any
  failure, which paints plain text. For a background that would show the creator's
  real room for the rest of the cut. The speaker matte holds the last good mask. If
  it never produced one, the frame renders without the background layer at all.
- **One recipe lane, absent when unused.** `speaker_background` is omitted from the
  recipe when None, so every recipe that does not use it keeps its digest.
- **The take records raw.** The camera previews the background live but records the
  original video, so the background stays editable and removable afterwards.

## Phase 0 spike (decided by measurement)

Questions, each answered by running something. Results are written here, not asked.

| Question | How | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Edge quality: `.balanced` vs `.accurate`, soft vs hard mask, with and without guided upsampling | Mac harness on three real talking-head clips (416 to 720 px wide, upscaled to 1080x1920), 2x crops over a magenta/cyan checker | **Measured (Mac).** `.accurate` + soft mask + steep curve `smoothstep(0.3, 0.7)` is cleanest: crisp, anti-aliased, keeps curls, no halo. The current text-matte recipe (balanced, mask shrunk to 192x256 then upscaled 5.6x, hard cut 0.40) leaves a wide pale halo and stair-steps. A hard cut on the accurate mask still chops curls. Vision's output size is set by quality (balanced 384x512, accurate 1512x2016), so input resolution changes nothing (mean mask difference 0.0026). A gray guided filter gave no visible gain. Low confidence on native-resolution footage, hair-color spill remains in every variant. |
| Mask rate: 15 Hz with hold vs 30 Hz vs 15 Hz with interpolation | Same harness on a moving subject, look for stepping | **Measured (Mac).** 15 Hz hold alternates about 5.7 px and 0 px silhouette movement per frame (freeze, then jump). Linear interpolation ghosts a double hand on fast motion. 30 Hz has neither. Decision: segment every frame. |
| Mac cost (not a device number) | Same harness | Vision per frame about 10.5 ms balanced and about 35 ms accurate, independent of input size. CI wrap and scale of the Vision buffer 0.8 ms, versus 1.5 ms for the `[Float]` CPU copy of the accurate mask. Keep the mask as a CIImage. |
| Cost: 60 s export, 30 fps preview, seek p95 | Physical iPhone 13 Pro (Release build, spike app `com.kria.app.dev.mattespike`): a 1800-frame simulated export (reader, render, Vision, composite, H.264 write) | **Measured (iPhone 13 Pro, 2026-10-31).** Vision per frame p50: balanced about 16 ms, accurate 77 to 86 ms. 60 s export: accurate 30 Hz 171 to 173 s (over budget, twice back to back), balanced 30 Hz 52 s, accurate 15 Hz 91 s, balanced 15 Hz 32 s. Thermal nominal to fair, no extra throttling on the second run. Peak memory about 245 MB (accurate), about 197 MB (balanced). CPU `[Float]` readback made no real difference. Decision: `.balanced`, every frame. The simulated export has no real compositor, so the full 60 s export on the shipped engine still needs a check (device test below). Seek p95 not measured. |
| Camera: can `AVCaptureMovieFileOutput` and `AVCaptureVideoDataOutput` run together | Physical iPhone 13 Pro, four 10 s runs (movie only control, data output only, data output + Vision balanced with a live matte preview, same with accurate) | **Measured (iPhone 13 Pro, 2026-10-31): yes.** In all four runs the movie finished with no error and recorded 307 frames at 30.3 fps with no frame gap over 40 ms, HEVC 1920x1080 with the portrait transform, and an audio track (no clipping, no session errors or interruptions). With balanced Vision: preview 30 fps, 301 frames in the window, no drops after startup (3 `FrameWasLate` total), Vision mean 25.6 ms, peak memory 134 MB, thermal nominal. With accurate: preview 10.2 fps and 198 dropped frames, so accurate is unusable for the live preview, but the recording itself was unaffected. The `AVAssetWriter` fallback is not needed. Audio levels differ between runs only because the speaker talked less in some runs (the data-only run was mostly silent speech), not because of the outputs. Not judged: how good the live preview looks to the eye. |

Device tests owed before the flag can be flipped (same standard as
`text-behind-subject-phone-failure-modes.md`): on an iPhone, a Talk-to-camera take
with a photo background, then a video background, must show the creator cleanly
cut out in both the preview and the export, a cutaway must cover the speaker, a
60 s export must finish within 120 s without a thermal step-down, and the
recording made with the live preview on must have clean audio.
