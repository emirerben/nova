## Problem Statement

Nova currently presents an open-ended chat surface but behaves like a narrow workflow. It often repeats the user's brief, then exposes a limitation or error instead of advancing the edit. That breaks the mental model established by ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini: the creator expects the system to interpret intent, use available capabilities, recover when one path is blocked, and continue toward a result.

This is not primarily a tone or copy defect. On `origin/main`, the pre-render Main Creator Agent is explicitly execution-free and can only ask one question, propose a strategy, or review a render. The post-render Edit Copilot has a much richer operation vocabulary, but its conversation and draft are browser-local, its turns are stateless server-side, and most operations are not available to the durable creation thread. The product therefore has two partial agents and no single owner of the creator's outcome.

Kria must become a domain agent for short-form video: one durable conversation that can inspect footage and the current edit, make editorial decisions, execute validated tools, show what changed, ask for approval at consequential boundaries, evaluate results, recover from failures, and continue until the creator has a publishable cut.

## Demand Evidence

- Nermin (`@nermozdemir`) creates lifestyle, Kinocha Matcha business, and travel content. Editing is painful enough that she sometimes considers not posting or quitting.
- Her fallback is CapCut. The workflow contains little useful AI and costs hours, yet she still returns to it because Nova does not reliably finish the job.
- Her expectation is set by ChatGPT and Claude: a conversational system should understand the assignment and help solve it, not repeat it back.
- The observed Nova failure is consistent: "It always restates the brief and then generally returns an issue or a limit."
- The dominant job is not ideation. It is building and polishing the cut from uploaded footage into something she is willing to publish.

## Status Quo

- `CreationThread` and `CreationThreadEvent` already provide a durable, append-only project conversation with revision fences, media attachment, render progress, and linked `PlanItem`, `Job`, and `CreatorAgentSession` state.
- The Main Creator Agent already has manifest- and context-hashed planning, explicit confirmation, idempotent execution receipts, render/review states, and a small server-executed craft bundle. Its model output is intentionally inert and cannot call tools.
- The creation UI renders every event as chat, but its confirmation card uses the original intent as its description. This makes repetition a visible product behavior, not only a prompt tendency.
- The Edit Copilot understands a broad editing vocabulary: timeline changes, text and caption edits, music and mix, overlays, SFX, looks, transitions, visual and motion blocks, speech-cut candidates, undo/repeat, and selected render operations.
- Copilot turns are stateless on the server. The browser sends an untrusted snapshot, applies most returned operations to local draft state, keeps conversation history in session storage, and reports best-effort execution receipts. The authoritative server only sees the edit at Save/render time.
- Capability flags and route-specific gates can cause an understood request to end in a canned unsupported, stale, or failed response. There is no general rescue policy that selects another valid path while preserving intent.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

The target user is a short-form creator who has raw phone footage and a publishing intent but does not want to operate a traditional editor. Nermin is the primary acceptance user because the failure is acute and observable.

The build is a full agent platform, not a Nermin-only workflow. The first production release must support Nova's existing creator formats—montage, narrated, talking-to-camera/subtitled, guided story, and supported mixed-media variants—through one continuous agent contract. Nermin's lifestyle, matcha-business, and travel footage is a mandatory real-world benchmark within that broader matrix.

## Constraints

- Keep Kria domain-bounded to short-form video creation and revision; do not imitate a general-purpose assistant outside that job.
- Reuse the existing render pipelines, editor-commit transaction, capability manifest, revision/ownership fences, idempotency receipts, and feature flags.
- Model output remains inert. Only server-owned, schema-validated tools may read or mutate project state or dispatch work; no storage paths, arbitrary commands, or raw renderer instructions enter the model boundary.
- Apply reversible, low-cost draft edits without confirmation. Require approval before a render, destructive or difficult-to-reverse change, or a genuinely ambiguous creative fork.
- Preserve the current asynchronous render model. The conversation must remain useful while work is queued, rendering, partially failed, or stale.
- Do not claim success from fluent text. Every claimed action must be backed by an execution receipt and reflected in the authoritative project snapshot.
- Maintain compatibility with in-flight creation threads, existing PlanItems/Jobs, saved editor drafts, and kill-switch rollback.

## Premises

- Kria owns the outcome from uploaded footage to a publishable first cut and subsequent revisions; it is not merely a brief collector or editor help panel.
- Every assistant turn must add at least one of: interpretation, a creative decision, a high-value question, a validated action, observable progress, evidence from the current result, or a viable recovery path. A paraphrase alone is never a valid turn.
- The current system should be unified rather than replaced. `CreationThread` remains the durable conversation/project spine; `CreatorAgentSession` remains the controller and budget owner; existing editor and craft contracts become tools behind one orchestrator.
- Orchestration alone is insufficient. The project must measure unsupported high-frequency creator requests and close bounded operation gaps when existing editor/render primitives cannot satisfy them.
- Honest boundaries remain visible, but the user-facing behavior is "best viable next move" rather than "limit first." Kria preserves the goal, explains the blocked step briefly, and offers or executes the closest safe path.
- One continuous agent conversation spans creation, render review, and editor revision. The editor is an inspectable workspace for the same state, not a second assistant with separate memory.
- Production quality must cover all supported creator formats. Nermin is a hard acceptance case, not an excuse to defer format parity.

## Cross-Model Perspective

An independent cold read strongly supported a domain video-editing agent that owns the result with consequential-action confirmations. Its sharpest formulation was: every turn must earn its place. It challenged an orchestration-only premise: if real creator requests repeatedly require transformations absent from the operation vocabulary, better conversation cannot compensate. The recommended fast validation is a tool loop over existing typed contracts, replayed against Nermin's actual workflow and a set of natural-language edit scripts, before expanding render primitives.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Conversation Patch

Improve prompts and chat copy, remove direct brief repetition, and add friendlier error messages. Rejected because it leaves the execution-free planner, browser-local copilot, split memory, and dead-end capability behavior intact.

### Approach B: Full Kria Domain-Agent Platform

Unify the creation thread, creator controller, edit operation catalog, server execution, render review, and recovery policy behind one durable conversational runtime. Chosen because it fixes the product contract and creates a base for expanding capabilities without accumulating more disconnected assistants.

### Approach C: Nermin-Only Vertical Slice

Build the general tool loop but ship only for lifestyle, matcha-business, and travel montage workflows. Rejected as the launch boundary because it would preserve different interaction models across Nova's formats. Retained as a mandatory benchmark and early validation fixture.

## Recommended Approach

Build a server-authoritative Kria runtime around the existing `CreationThread` and `CreatorAgentSession` graph.

Each user turn resolves a trusted project snapshot and capability manifest, then asks the model for a typed turn plan containing a user-facing response plus zero or more tool intents. A deterministic policy layer validates tool eligibility, target pins, cost/risk class, budgets, and confirmation requirements. Safe draft tools execute immediately and append receipts; consequential tools create an approval request; long-running tools append progress and resume the same turn loop when their result arrives. The agent then observes the updated authoritative state before claiming an outcome.

The tool registry should expose existing capabilities rather than duplicate their business logic: creation setup and media selection; strategy compilation; editor-draft operations; atomic editor commit and rerender; current craft commands; variant selection; render status and review; retry/recovery; and narrowly allowlisted asset/catalog lookup. The rich Edit Copilot vocabulary becomes a shared typed operation layer. Browser-only application becomes a compatibility adapter during migration, then the durable agent draft/receipt becomes the source of truth for agent-authored edits.

Responses use a conversational behavior contract rather than canned stage copy. Kria states the editorial judgment or completed change first, grounds it in footage/edit evidence where useful, and gives one clear next move. When blocked, it classifies the failure as retryable, stale, unavailable, ambiguous, or unsupported; refreshes state or tries an allowed alternative when possible; and only surfaces the boundary after offering the rescue.

Capability development becomes evidence-driven. Store privacy-safe intent categories, selected tools, rejection reasons, rescue outcome, and whether the user subsequently opened the manual editor or abandoned the task. Review the highest-frequency unsupported intents against creator impact. The platform launch instruments these gaps; new render primitives remain a separately prioritized roadmap unless an existing-format acceptance scenario cannot be completed without one.

Roll out behind a new unified-agent gate with shadow planning first and internal execution next. Preserve the current chat-first creation path as rollback until the full format matrix, migration behavior, and outcome metrics pass. No external cohort receives the unified runtime until all currently supported creator formats pass the release matrix; internal implementation may progress format by format.

## Authoritative State and Runtime Contract

| Concern | Source of truth | Role of other records |
|---|---|---|
| Conversation and user intent | `CreationThreadEvent` ordered by thread sequence | `CreationThread.state` is a rebuildable projection for fast reads, never an execution source |
| Agent workflow, budgets, and active target | `CreatorAgentSession` revision and ownership epoch | Thread state mirrors status for UI only |
| Working, not-yet-rendered edit | New versioned server-side `CreatorEditDraft`, keyed by item and variant with `base_generation` and monotonic `draft_revision` | Browser state is a cache/projection of this draft during migration |
| Rendered/committed edit | Exact `Job` variant and `render_generation_id` selected by the `PlanItem` | Drafts point to this generation and must rebase or fail when it changes |
| Claimed side effect | Successful, idempotent `CreatorAgentExecution` receipt tied to exact thread/session/draft/job pins | Assistant text may summarize only receipts observed after execution |

The runtime extends the existing creator-session states rather than creating a parallel workflow:

1. `briefing/awaiting_feedback` → accept one sequenced user turn and acquire a short-lived per-thread turn lease.
2. `planning` → resolve the authoritative snapshot and capability manifest; create a typed plan against exact revisions.
3. Safe read/draft work → atomically prevalidate and apply one operation bundle to `CreatorEditDraft`; append receipts; return to `awaiting_feedback`.
4. Consequential work → persist an `awaiting_confirmation` approval pinned to the exact session, draft, job, generation, manifest, and ownership revisions.
5. On approval, `executing` commits the approved draft through the existing editor-commit/creator-craft boundary, then `rendering`; worker events resume reconciliation and `reviewing` against the exact new generation.
6. A successful review returns to `awaiting_feedback` with evidence and a concrete next move. Exhausted or nonrecoverable failure enters `blocked` with preserved state and a specific manual or retry handoff; it never loops silently.

One thread processes one mutating control-plane turn at a time. A newer message supersedes a turn only before a side-effect receipt is committed. The lease is released as soon as an async action is durably dispatched. While a render runs, Kria may answer status/help questions and interpret one queued successor intent against read-only evidence, but it may not mutate or dispatch against the in-flight generation. When the render settles, the runtime resolves a fresh snapshot and replans the queued intent; it never carries forward precomputed operations.

Cross-device draft writes use `draft_revision` compare-and-swap. A conflict refreshes state and replans; v1 never guesses at a merge. Duplicate tool calls, approvals, and callbacks replay their existing receipt. The turn lease is a database-backed epoch token with a 15-second TTL and 5-second heartbeat. A process may take over only after expiry; the new holder first reconciles every `running` execution by idempotency key and external job identity before issuing another call.

Cancellation is a first-class thread action. Pending approvals and queued successor intents cancel synchronously and never execute. Planning cancellation revokes the lease epoch; any later result fails its fence. Draft mutation can be undone head-first but not interrupted after commit. Render cancellation is one-click and best-effort after dispatch: the request and its receipt are durable, a worker completion that wins the race is retained as an unselected result, and Kria reports the actual terminal outcome instead of claiming cancellation. Cancellation itself never starts another render.

## Tool, Approval, and Failure Policy

The launch registry is versioned and contains only these existing capability families: project/thread reads; media and edit inspection; creation format and strategy setup; draft timeline/text/caption/music/mix/overlay/SFX/look/transition/visual/motion operations proven portable by audit; atomic editor commit; current creator-craft commands; render dispatch/status/review/retry; variant selection; and allowlisted catalog lookup. Navigation-only operations remain client actions. Unsupported operations are not exposed as tools.

Every editor operation is classified before migration:

- `portable`: deterministic from the authoritative snapshot and executable on the server; available to Kria.
- `adapter-required`: depends on a current client calculation; it may produce a durable `client_proposal_receipt`, but Kria cannot claim it executed. If the server can reconstruct and validate the resulting post-state, Save promotes it to an authoritative draft receipt; otherwise it is navigation/manual-only.
- `unsupported`: absent or unsafe; omitted from the registry and counted in capability-gap telemetry.

The risk matrix is fixed by tool metadata, not model judgment:

- Reads, analysis-cache lookup, and reversible draft mutations run immediately.
- A draft bundle is prevalidated as a unit and committed atomically. V1 Undo is head-only: it copies the immediately previous immutable snapshot into a new compare-and-swap revision, so it cannot erase unrelated later work or move history backward.
- Editor commit, render/rerender, source/media removal, and any difficult-to-reverse mutation require approval. Reverting a committed render means restoring a prior draft snapshot and approving a new render.
- External publishing is not a launch capability and is out of scope.
- An approval groups one exact consequential action, expires on any pinned-state change or after 30 minutes, denial closes it without mutation, and duplicate approval is idempotent.

Multi-tool plans execute dependency groups in order. All synchronous operations in a draft group validate before any write. Async actions start only after their prerequisite receipt commits. A later-group failure does not erase an earlier successful group; the assistant names the completed and failed portions from receipts and offers Undo, Retry, or Continue. It never describes a whole bundle as complete after partial success.

Default control-plane ceilings are configurable but fail closed: two model continuation rounds and eight tool calls per user turn; one automatic retry for a proven transient synchronous failure; one render dispatch per approval; and 30 seconds of synchronous orchestration before yielding progress. Async render time is outside the turn wall clock and resumes from durable events. Existing per-session question, agent, render, and iteration budgets remain authoritative. Budget exhaustion yields one terminal recovery response, not another model loop.

Recovery follows a deterministic order: refresh stale state and replan; reconcile an ambiguous receipt before retrying; retry a transient failure once; choose a lower-cost/quality-preserving exposed tool only when it still satisfies the user's stated intent; otherwise ask one decision-changing question or explain the blocked capability with the closest valid manual action. Any material quality loss or extra render requires confirmation.

## Turn Protocol and Evidence

A model-authored turn is inert and schema-validates to: `message`, `evidence_refs`, ordered `tool_intents` with dependencies, and an optional `approval_summary`. The server adds immutable target pins, risk class, and idempotency keys; the model cannot author them. After execution, the runtime performs an observation pass over receipts and the refreshed snapshot before emitting a completion message.

Trusted planning evidence consists of opaque media IDs plus cached, versioned clip metadata; transcript/audio summaries; the current draft/render structure; capability availability; user-authored conversation; and, after a render, bounded quality-review evidence linked to the exact generation. Raw footage/transcript text is always opaque quoted data, not an instruction channel. Executor authorization, tool availability, target validation, and approval policy are enforced outside the model regardless of what media-derived text says.

Every response must satisfy one machine-checkable `turn_value` category: `interpretation`, `creative_decision`, `question`, `action_result`, `progress`, `review_evidence`, or `recovery`. The category must reference its supporting decision, question, receipt, status, evidence, or recovery payload; labeled evals enforce the contract. Semantic similarity to the user's message is telemetry only, because similarity alone cannot distinguish a useful concise acknowledgement from padded repetition. Action-result language is generated from observed receipts, not the planned intents.

The normative v1 protocol has two separate envelopes. `TurnPlan` is inert and never claims that work happened:

```json
{
  "schema_version": 1,
  "mode": "act",
  "direct_response": null,
  "evidence_refs": ["clip:7:product_reveal", "draft:12"],
  "tool_intents": [
    {"intent_id": "i1", "tool": "apply_draft_bundle", "arguments": {"operations": []}, "depends_on": []}
  ],
  "approval_summary": "Move the product reveal earlier and tighten the pauses, then render a new cut."
}
```

`mode = respond` requires a `direct_response` with `turn_value = interpretation|creative_decision|question|progress`; it has no mutating intents and displays immediately. `mode = act` displays no model-authored completion copy before execution; safe work uses the thinking/progress treatment, while consequential work displays only the prospective `approval_summary`. After tools settle, a separate `ObservedTurnResponse` displays:

```json
{
  "schema_version": 1,
  "message": "I moved the product reveal ahead of the setup and tightened the pauses.",
  "turn_value": {"kind": "action_result", "receipt_ids": ["exec_123"]},
  "evidence_refs": ["draft:13"],
  "next_actions": ["undo", "request_render"]
}
```

Observed responses allow `action_result|progress|review_evidence|recovery`, cite only settled or durably running receipts, and contain no new tool intents. A further tool step requires another validated `TurnPlan` continuation within the turn budget. The server materializes each intent as a pinned execution with `status = pending|running|succeeded|failed|cancelled|outcome_unknown`, `risk = read|draft|render|destructive`, dependency IDs, idempotency key, exact thread/session/draft/job/generation/manifest/ownership pins, timestamps, a typed result or typed `{code, retryable, detail}` error, and `observed_by_event_id`. Approval records contain the grouped intent IDs, exact pins, expiry, and `status = pending|approved|denied|expired|cancelled|consumed`. Stale pins never execute; they produce a `stale` observation and at most one fresh replan. Duplicate callbacks update only the matching execution and generation and cannot append a second completion event.

Post-render evidence is deliberately bounded: an exact-generation contact sheet of at most 12 frames covering the first three seconds and shot boundaries; transcript/caption timing and geometry checks; loudness, peak, silence, and A/V-duration metrics; and renderer/job integrity signals. A versioned multimodal reviewer may reason over that package, but claims cite the underlying evidence IDs. Cache keys include job, variant, generation, evidence-producer version, and reviewer version. Evidence failure is nonfatal: Kria reports that review evidence is unavailable and does not invent a quality judgment.

User-visible events are also fixed:

| Durable event | Chat treatment |
|---|---|
| planning | transient thinking treatment; restored as quiet status after refresh, not a permanent speech bubble |
| draft applied / undone | assistant outcome plus compact, inspectable change card and Undo when eligible |
| approval requested / expired / denied | one action card pinned to exact state; expired cards disable and offer Replan |
| async dispatched / progress | one updating status card; repeated worker callbacks update it rather than adding bubbles |
| queued successor intent | assistant interpretation plus “after this render” marker; one queued intent may be replaced or cancelled |
| partial success / failure / reconciliation | one receipt-backed outcome naming what changed and the next valid action |
| render reviewed / ready | playable result, evidence-backed review, and one clear next move |

Late progress for an older generation updates its historical card only and never steals focus from a newer turn.

V1 ownership stops at a first cut plus user-directed revisions. Every completed render gets one automatic evidence review, but Kria does not autonomously rerender; it may propose one bounded improvement and must obtain a new render approval. The session ends when the creator accepts/selects a result, leaves explicitly, or reaches a terminal budget/capability state. Proactive multi-render polishing remains out of scope.

## Migration and Release Gates

1. **Operation portability audit:** freeze and classify the current Copilot catalog; create cross-format fixtures; define shared tool metadata and turn-value evals. No user-visible change.
2. **Shadow orchestrator:** run typed plans against production-like snapshots with all tools side-effect-disabled. Persist only redacted comparison telemetry; do not show shadow text or perform extra paid media analysis. Promotion requires zero unsafe/unauthorized intents, 100% valid turn-value evidence, and at least 90% exact human-labeled intent+target and tool-selection match on the same minimum 30 scenarios for every format token.
3. **Durable draft internally:** add `CreatorEditDraft`, server execution for portable operations, exact receipts, async continuation, and browser projection. Internal users exercise each format; legacy chat/copilot remains canonical when the gate is off.
4. **All-format cohort release:** enable the unified conversation for named users only after every supported format passes creation, edit, confirmed render, stale/conflict, async recovery, and rollback scenarios. Adapter-required operations visibly open the same editor draft rather than starting a separate copilot history.
5. **Cutover:** make the server draft canonical for agent-authored work, retire session-storage conversation ownership, and retain versioned read adapters for old clients/threads. Rollback disables new planning/execution while old clients continue reading committed Job state and compatible projected drafts. Reconciliation tooling reports orphaned approvals, drafts, and executions before broader rollout.

Every phase has a kill switch and preserves backward-readable records. No phase dual-writes two canonical edit states. Shadow state is disposable; internal and cohort phases always identify whether the legacy or unified gate owns a thread when it is created. Any adapter used for a launch-critical scenario must be eliminated before cohort release. A noncritical manual-only handoff is a temporary degraded state: it opens the same draft, returns to the same thread after server-validated Save, and does not count as agent completion; each such operation has a tracked removal gate.

The canonical production tokens are `montage`, `talking_head`, `day_vlog`, `single_hero`, `subtitled`, `narrated`, `narrated_planned`, and `narrated_ready`. Product-facing Montage maps to `montage`; Guided Story maps to `day_vlog` or `single_hero` according to the selected archetype; Talking to camera maps to `talking_head` or `subtitled` according to caption/audio treatment; Narrated maps to one of the three narrated lifecycle tokens. Mixed media is an input condition on a parent token, not a ninth format. The cohort gate freezes this minimum contract; optional feature-flagged effects do not block launch unless the flag is enabled for the cohort:

| Format tokens | Required operation subset beyond universal inspect/strategy/undo/commit/render/status/review/retry/select |
|---|---|
| `montage` | reorder/remove/trim/split/duration, text/title, music/mix, transition, look |
| `day_vlog`, `single_hero` | guided timeline edits, text/title, transition, look |
| `talking_head`, `subtitled` | caption text/timing/style, speech-cut candidate, output trim, mix |
| `narrated`, `narrated_planned`, `narrated_ready` | voiceover readiness, supporting timeline, text/title, voice/background-music mix |
| mixed video/image inputs within any eligible format | add unused sources, image duration, image stacking while preserving the parent format's required subset |

Each token runs with its production-intended feature flags and has at least 30 labeled behavioral scenarios, including creation, one reversible draft bundle, one confirmed render, head-only Undo, stale and cross-device conflict, queued intent during render, cancellation, partial failure, retry/reconciliation, and rollback. All mandatory safety/recovery scenarios must pass; at least 27 of 30 manifest-supported requests must execute successfully; every failed case must end in a specific, valid recovery rather than a generic limit/error. Product owns the scenario labels; engineering owns invariants and fixtures; design owns conversational and usability adjudication. The operation-portability audit may remove nonessential operations from the registry, but it may not weaken this minimum subset without a new product decision.

## Security, Privacy, and Evaluation Definitions

- Every tool rechecks authenticated creator ownership, thread/session/item/job linkage, manifest/context hashes, and target revisions. The registry passes opaque IDs only; storage credentials, paths, signed URLs, arbitrary code, and raw renderer graphs never enter model output.
- Footage, transcripts, catalog labels, and external metadata are untrusted prompt content. Prompt-injection tests verify they cannot select tools, bypass confirmation, expand authorization, or alter policy.
- Observability stores intent taxonomy, tool/receipt metadata, latency, and rejection classes with content minimized and redacted. Raw prompts, transcripts, frames, and media are excluded by default.
- Replay media requires explicit creator consent, access control, a recorded purpose, and a retention/deletion date. Revocation deletes the media and derived frames/transcripts; non-media structural fixtures may remain only after irreversible de-identification.
- “Supported request” means the required operation is present and available in the resolved manifest for that exact snapshot. “Understood” means the human-labeled intent and target match the agent's parsed intent. “Generic limit/error ending” means a terminal turn without a specific tool receipt, retry, state refresh, decision-changing question, or valid manual handoff.
- “Publishable” is determined by the creator choosing the result for posting, supplemented by a blind human rubric for hook clarity, continuity, legibility, audio balance, and absence of render defects. “Hands-on time” measures active interaction, excluding upload, queue, and render waits.
- Report control-plane latency separately from upload, queue, and render latency. The five-minute target is measured from upload completion to first playable cut at p50 for the agreed input-duration/device/network class; also report p90 and each latency component so the SLO remains falsifiable.
- Launch evaluation uses at least 30 labeled natural-language scenarios per creator-format token, every required recovery scenario, and three consented Nermin projects. Every safety, authorization, stale-state, cancellation, and rollback scenario must pass. Per token, execution completion must be at least 27/30 and specific recovery coverage among failures must be 100%. Across a separate corpus of at least 200 manifest-supported requests, execution completion alone must be at least 90% with a Wilson 95% lower bound of at least 85%. A separate injected-failure corpus of at least 40 cases must achieve at least 95% specific-recovery coverage with a Wilson lower bound of at least 85%. Generic terminal limit/error endings must be below 5% with an upper bound below 8%. One infrastructure-only failure may be rerun once; a repeat counts as a failure. No aggregate may hide a failing format.
- Latency uses a separate corpus of at least 50 renders, with at least eight per format family, split across warm/cold evidence caches and nominal/elevated queue load. The standard input class is 3–10 uploaded clips, no clip over two minutes, and no more than ten minutes total source duration; timing begins after upload completes. Release requires first playable cut p50 under five minutes and p90 under ten minutes while also reporting control-plane, queue, evidence, and render components.

## Interaction Design Contract

The conversation is an outcome ledger, not an event log. The durable visible sequence is the creator's request, Kria's value-adding decision/result/recovery, and—only when needed—an artifact that proves a change or enables the next state. Planning, retries, tool selection, and worker callbacks are transient status, never conversational bubbles.

Cards are reserved for playable media, exact-state approvals, inspectable draft receipts, and actionable recovery. Direct answers and creative judgments remain compact assistant turns. One lifecycle artifact per exact render generation mutates from approval through progress to ready/partial/failure; older generations stay historical and inert. Draft cards show at most three high-impact changes, then View all changes; Undo appears only for the current head revision.

Approval copy names the proposed editorial decision and consequence. It never repeats `CreationThread.state.intent`. Completion language exists only after the server observes authoritative receipts. While rendering, the composer stays usable for status/help and one replaceable or cancellable “After this render” intent. Render completion never auto-opens the editor.

Desktop retains the 260px project rail, full-width pre-render conversation, and 420px conversation rail next to `EditorShell` after a cut exists. Mobile retains Projects sheet plus explicit Chat/Editor tabs. The composer and its draft survive tab changes, offline recovery, and recoverable errors.

Auto-scroll only when the creator is within 80px of the live edge or just sent a message; otherwise show a New update control without moving focus. The transcript announces only new semantic turns, while one mutable `role=status` region announces active phase changes once. Late historical-generation updates do not steal focus or announce as the active outcome. All controls retain 44px targets, visible focus at 200% zoom, non-color status labels, reduced-motion behavior, and logical focus restoration after approve, undo, cancel, expiry, or conflict.

Assistant copy must lead with exactly one of: editorial decision, observed action result, decision-changing question, useful progress, evidence-backed review, or concrete recovery. It omits filler acknowledgement, “I understand you want…,” raw codes, invented completion, generic failure language, and policy explanations before a rescue. The turn ends with at most one recommended next move.

## Open Questions

None at product-decision level. Exact model choice and rollout percentages are operational parameters selected from benchmark data while retaining config-level kill switches.

## Success Criteria

- On a multi-turn behavioral eval suite, 0 assistant turns merely paraphrase the latest user message; every turn satisfies the turn-value contract.
- A creator can start with raw footage and complete creation, review, and revisions in the same durable conversation across refresh and device changes; cross-device conflicts refresh and replan without lost committed work.
- All currently supported creator formats pass end-to-end agent scenarios for initial creation, one reversible draft edit, one confirmed rerender, stale-state recovery, and a capability failure.
- On Nermin's real lifestyle, matcha-business, and travel footage, Kria reaches a publishable result with under 30 minutes of creator hands-on time and no required CapCut escape. For standard-class projects, first playable cut latency is under five minutes at p50 and under ten minutes at p90 from upload completion.
- Claims such as "I changed," "I saved," or "I rendered" are always backed by an authoritative execution receipt and current-state verification.
- At least 90% of understood, manifest-supported requests execute successfully, independent of the recovery metric. When execution fails, at least 95% receive a specific valid recovery; generic limit/error endings remain under 5% of turns, measured per format as well as in aggregate.
- Render, ownership, idempotency, and stale-generation invariants remain intact; no model-authored storage capability or unvalidated render instruction crosses the execution boundary.
- The team can rank unsupported intent categories by frequency and creator impact and trace each one to the exact capability/policy rejection that caused it.

## Dependencies

- Existing creation-thread and creator-session persistence, editor-commit validation, render/review jobs, edit-operation parser and goldens, capability manifests, and feature-flag infrastructure.
- A server-authoritative agent draft/operation receipt that can coexist with the current browser-local editor during migration.
- Cross-format fixtures and real creator footage with consent for evaluation; production media must remain outside git and existing retention policies.
- Model and tool-loop observability that separates interpretation quality, policy rejection, executor failure, render failure, and user abandonment.
