# Edit story

Start with what the creator wants a viewer to understand or feel and the actual
source evidence. Use only the guidance for the current format and your assigned
planning role. This procedure does not authorize switching formats, approving
wording, or adding unsupported operations. Account themes and a proposed story
are intent, not proof of what appears in the footage.

## Montage

Give each chosen beat a job such as orientation, action, contrast, reaction, or
payoff. Build a causal or spatial progression when the footage supports it.
A purely visual montage can follow movement, texture, or atmosphere without an
explanation or added text. Do not manufacture a lesson or a reveal. Prefer
meaningful variety to interchangeable shots. Hold an action long enough to register;
choose pacing from the material rather than a universal seconds-per-cut rule.
When a creator song or timing is fixed, develop the progression within that timing.

## Talking and source speech

Preserve the speaker's point, including negation and qualifications. Tighten only
where authorized and where the complete thought survives. Keep the requested
speaker and source audio. Subtitles represent retained speech rather than rewriting
it as a hook. Cutaways can illustrate a point without replacing or interrupting
continuous voice. Do not insert silent montage gaps into a continuous-voice request.
For requested excerpts, choose complete passages and follow the creator's pattern
of speaker views and cutaways. A stronger opening never licenses changing a quote.

## Narrated video

Treat the supplied recorded narration and its ordered timed beats as authoritative.
Choose visuals that support each spoken action or idea. Use a supplied relevant
source moment when available rather than an arbitrary start. Do not reorder or
rewrite narration to fit convenient footage. Missing visual evidence calls for the
caller's supported omission or clarification behavior, not a fabricated match.
An illustrative shot is not evidence of an unstated location, outcome, or event.
The caller owns exact timing unless its schema explicitly delegates that choice.

## Slide post

Separate the cover decision from the order in which the story unfolds. Choose an
informative cover when the creator delegates it. Use progression, contrast, or
process only when supported by the supplied media and request. Respect an explicit
cover and fixed sequence. Keep every asset when the caller requires complete
coverage, including both photos and videos. Improve their ordering rather than
dropping a weaker asset. Do not invent a final result or explanatory copy to connect
unrelated images. A caption or additional text is a separate authorized decision.

## Preserve the request

Creator constraints outrank editorial defaults. Preserve requested source inclusion,
order, exact timing, approved text, audible speech, and song synchronization.
A fixed sequence is not permission to reorder for a stronger hook. Do not invent
source moments, missing dialogue, unsupported effects, or exact source intervals
from vague summaries. Ask only when missing evidence prevents a meaningful choice
and the caller permits clarification; otherwise keep the proposal modest.

Make important choices in executable fields supported by the current capability
manifest and output schema. A promise in summary or rationale does not create a
cut, overlay, or sound. If the chosen path cannot express a requirement, use its
existing refusal or clarification contract instead of promising it.
