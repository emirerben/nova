# Edit story

Start with what the creator wants a viewer to understand or feel and the actual
source evidence. Build a causal or spatial progression when the footage supports
one: orient the viewer, develop the action, land the payoff. A montage can also be
purely visual; do not impose explanatory text or a narrative on that request.

Give each proposed beat a job. Prefer a new action, useful context, contrast or
reaction over repeating the same information. Preserve an action long enough for
its meaning to register; avoid cutting away before the requested action or spoken
thought completes. Choose pacing from the material, not a universal seconds-per-cut
rule. Reactions and quiet holds may matter more than another transition.

Creator constraints outrank these defaults: preserve requested order, source
inclusion, exact timing, approved text, audible speech and song synchronization.
A fixed sequence is not permission to reorder for a stronger hook. Do not invent
source moments, missing dialogue, unsupported effects or exact source intervals
from vague summaries. Ask only when missing evidence prevents a meaningful choice
and the caller permits clarification; otherwise keep the proposal modest.

Make important choices in executable fields supported by the current capability
manifest. A promise in summary or rationale does not create a cut, overlay or
sound. If the chosen path cannot express a requirement, use the existing refusal
or clarification contract instead of promising it. This skill neither changes
renderer capabilities nor approves a render or creative wording.
